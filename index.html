About Me

I am Juliet Ibrahim, an Urban Planner, Data Analyst, and Climate Resilience Advocate. My work focuses on building sustainable cities, applying ecohydrological principles to flood management, and using data-driven insights to address climate change, guide urban development, and strengthen healthcare systems.

My research interests focus on climate adaptation and resilient urban planning, participatory land use planning, and flood risk management using ecohydrological approaches. I am also engaged in exploring land governance and relocation planning in vulnerable communities, environmental justice and adaptation policy, and the dynamics of urban informality and infrastructure governance in the Global South.

Perception and Attitude of Women Towards Family Planning in Narayi Community

Conducted a research project assessing awareness, perceptions, and challenges women face in accessing family planning services in Narayi Community. Using Kobo Tool, Power BI, Power Query, and PowerPoint, the study analyzed survey data to uncover key insights:

  • 87.04% of women were aware of family planning methods, while 12.96% were not.
  • Implants, natural methods, condoms, and pills were the most commonly known methods.
  • Internet (100%), family/friends (99%), and healthcare providers (64%) were major sources of information.
  • Challenges included partner opposition (27%), distance (24%), cost and cultural/religious beliefs (19%), and lack of information (10%).
  • Perception: 44% said family planning is very accessible, while 3% said not at all accessible.

The project concluded with recommendations to increase awareness, address cultural barriers, and improve healthcare access for women in Narayi Community.

Publications

Flood Control in Kaduna South L.G.A

Published in IIARD International Journal of Geography & Environmental Management (Vol. 9, No. 5, 2023). This study applied Ecohydrological principles to address annual flooding in Barnawa, Down Quarters, and Chenchengi Layout. Using GIS tools, the research identified affected structures and farmland, and recommended relocation of developments along the River Kaduna bank, alongside strict enforcement of flood management policies.

How Data Analytics Drives Climate Action

Published on Feb 4, 2026 by Juliet Ibrahim

Climate change is one of the greatest challenges of our time, and tackling it requires more than passion—it demands precision. This is where data analytics comes in. By transforming raw information into actionable insights, data analytics empowers governments, organizations, and communities to make smarter decisions for a sustainable future.

Turning Numbers into Narratives

Every dataset tells a story. From satellite imagery tracking deforestation to temperature records showing rising heat waves, analytics helps us interpret these signals. Tools like Power BI, SQL, and Python allow climate experts to visualize trends, identify risks, and communicate findings in ways that inspire action.

Predicting Climate Risks

Predictive models are changing the game. By analyzing historical data, we can forecast floods, droughts, and extreme weather events with greater accuracy. This enables communities to prepare in advance, saving lives and reducing economic losses.

Driving Policy and Advocacy

Policymakers rely on evidence to craft effective climate strategies. Data analytics provides that evidence—whether it’s showing the impact of emissions on air quality or mapping vulnerable populations. With clear, data-backed insights, advocacy becomes more persuasive and policies more impactful.

Empowering Local Communities

Beyond global conferences and government reports, data analytics also empowers local communities. Farmers can use climate data to plan crop cycles, while city planners can design greener infrastructure. When data is shared openly, it becomes a tool for collective resilience.

Conclusion

Climate action is no longer just about raising awareness—it’s about making informed, strategic choices. Data analytics bridges the gap between knowledge and action, turning information into solutions. As technology advances, its role in building a sustainable future will only grow stronger.
